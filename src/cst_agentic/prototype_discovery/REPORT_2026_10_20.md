### Important transcript details

- **Next request versus next user message:** in the uncertainty case, schemas
  were already present on the second request. Qwen nevertheless asked: “Please
  send the next message (e.g., 'go') and I will execute.” The discovery wording
  appears liable to cause an unnecessary approval boundary. The model's promise
  not to retry is not evidence of handling an injected uncertain operation.
- **Preserve means preserve:** the edit case explicitly passed
  `solver_type="Time Domain"`. Its final answer admitted this was an interpretation
  of “verified solver setup” and asked the user to flag a mismatch **after** the
  mock action. The fixture did not establish that solver family. This violates
  preserving setup and agreeing missing engineering choices before execution.
  Inspection was also deferred to an optional follow-up rather than performed.
- **Valid arguments are not valid behavior:** the wait used `max_wait_s=55`,
  legal under the schema but not validated against the application's real
  transport/queue budget. The mock returned immediately; no responsiveness or
  safe stopping conclusion is possible.
- **No native-completion overclaim in the edit summary:** it distinguished the
  synthetic activity transition from numerical validity. That positive reporting
  behavior does not excuse the invented solver or absent readback.
- **Bundled database versus native library:** construction first searched a
  synthetic bundled list for exact library names, then loaded the named entries.
  Bundled membership is not proof of native library availability. This mock
  deliberately simplified both lists and ignored category filtering, so search
  quality and native lookup correctness cannot be inferred.
- **Parameter/port limits were discussed but not exercised:** both construction
  plans offered to pause rather than silently freeze numeric port coordinates.
  Neither reached the port schema/call. No expression-driven brick was built and
  no stored-expression readback was tested.

## Grouping comparison: exploratory, not a winner

Inspection-first reduced the cylinder surface from **13 to 10 visible functions**
and prompt tokens from **22,627 to 19,632**. Both spent five requests; proposal-style
returned a summary while inspection-first ended at creation acceptance. The caps
were six versus five, respectively, so completion is **not a fair pass-rate comparison**.

Construction had equal six-turn caps. Inspection-first used fewer prompt tokens
(18,411 versus 23,299) but made two oversized selections versus one and took a
redundant connection route. It reached parameters, while proposal-style reached
material loading. Neither built geometry. Lower token cost is not better behavior.
These are single samples of a bundled variant (startup plus two group changes),
not a causal study of one boundary or evidence of an optimal count cap.

The group descriptions/counts did not prevent broad over-budget choices. Across
the run, four unprompted oversized selections occurred in addition to the two
deliberately requested rejection tests. Host atomic rejection prevented silent
truncation. There was no unbounded discovery loop, but focused construction spent
half its six turns selecting tools. Full replacement was accepted after another
active capability in proposal construction, but no subsequent request carrying
the replacement occurred before its cap; that live wire transition remains unproven.

## Recommendations for the discovery decision

1. Retain explicit groups as a viable **mechanism candidate**; do not accept the
   current controller behavior as implementation-ready.
2. Clarify that activation is for the **next internal model request in the same
   user task**, not permission to wait for another user message. A complete
   authorized instruction should proceed without a second “go.”
3. Return deterministic deduplicated counts, rejection reasons and compact valid
   selection examples; investigate narrower groups for project creation versus
   connection/lifecycle, and for library loading versus unrelated material edits.
   These are hypotheses for the discovery ticket, not a silently adopted design.
4. Keep host-side allowlists/atomic activation and capability-aware safeguards.
   Enforce preservation of known setup; do not rely on post-hoc prose admitting an
   unapproved choice. A narrower instruction-following role still must not invent
   a solver, skip required inspection or misrepresent evidence.
5. Use realistic, predeclared per-task budgets and equal comparative caps. Report
   partial effects at a cap rather than treating all returned turns as success.
6. Cover relevant exposed mock operations. The redundant connection block is a
   harness gap; it must not be counted as native connection failure. Do not relax
   real safety checks to obtain a prettier prototype result.

Further evidence is required for actual uncertainty handling, completed expression-
driven construction, port-dependency disclosure, supported/unsupported edit closure,
stop semantics, material absence, and unsolicited engineering proposals/trade-offs.
This trial does not establish either engineering-assistant viability or that a
narrower role would solve the observed failures. All connected acceptance work
remains separate and requires an explicitly agreed disposable CST target.
